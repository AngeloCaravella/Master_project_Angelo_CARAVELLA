### 1. DDPG (Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient)

DDPG is like teaching a robot to perform a task. It has two key parts: the "Actor" and the "Critic".

*   **The Actor (The Player):** Looks at the current situation (state) and decides on an action to take. Its goal is to get the best possible score.
*   **The Critic (The Coach):** Watches the Actor take an action and evaluates how good that action was in that situation.

The Actor adjusts its strategy based on the Critic's feedback, trying to perform actions that the Critic will rate highly. This process repeats, and over time, the Actor gets better at its task.

```mermaid

    subgraph DDPG["DDPG: The Player and the Coach"]
        direction LR
        subgraph Critic["The Coach (Critic Network)"]
            A1["1. Look at a past move (state, action)"] --> A2;
            A2["2. See the outcome (reward) and what a 'stable' version of the coach thinks of the *next* move"] --> A3;

        subgraph Actor["The Player (Actor Network)"]

        end

        subgraph Target["The Stable Replay (Target Networks)"]

        end
    end
```

### 2. SAC (Soft Actor-Critic)

SAC is a more advanced version of an Actor-Critic algorithm. It also has a "player" and a "coach", but with a key difference: it encourages exploration.

*   **The Goal:** SAC tries to maximize two things at once: the expected reward (like DDPG) AND the **entropy** of its policy.
*   **Entropy:** This is a measure of randomness or unpredictability. By trying to maximize entropy, the Actor is rewarded for trying out a wide variety of actions and not always sticking to the same old strategy.

This makes the agent a better explorer, which can help it discover new and better ways to solve a task. An `alpha` parameter is used to control the trade-off between getting high scores and being exploratory.

```mermaid
graph TD
    subgraph SAC["SAC: The Creative Player"]
        direction TB
        S1["Like DDPG, has an Actor (player) and a Critic (coach)."] --> S2;
        S2["<b>Key Difference:</b> The Actor is also rewarded for being unpredictable and trying new things (this is called 'entropy')."] --> S3;
        S3["The goal is to maximize both the score (reward) AND unpredictability (entropy)."] --> S4;
        S4["An 'alpha' value balances these two goals.<br/>High alpha = more exploration.<br/>Low alpha = more focus on high scores."]
    end
```

### 3. DDPG + PER (Prioritized Experience Replay)

This is not a new algorithm, but a powerful upgrade to how DDPG (or other algorithms) learn from their memories.

*   **Experience Replay:** Agents store their past experiences (state, action, reward, next_state) in a memory buffer and learn by sampling random batches from it.
*   **The Problem:** In a standard buffer, every memory has an equal chance of being picked. However, some memories are much more valuable for learning than others.
*   **PER's Solution:** Prioritized Experience Replay gives a "priority score" to each memory. The more "surprising" an experience was (i.e., the bigger the error in the Critic's prediction), the higher its priority. When it's time to learn, the agent focuses on the high-priority memories.

This is like a student focusing their study time on the exam questions they got wrong, instead of re-reading the entire textbook. It makes learning much more efficient.

```mermaid
graph TD
    subgraph PER["PER: Learning from Surprising Moments"]
        direction TB
        P1["The Replay Buffer is a memory of past experiences."] --> P2;
        P2["<b>Standard Method:</b> Pick random memories to learn from."] --> P3;
        P3["<b>PER's Method:</b><br/>1. Give a 'priority score' to each memory.<br/>High score = The outcome was very surprising or unexpected."] --> P4;
        P4["2. When learning, focus on the memories with the highest priority scores."] --> P5;
        P5["This makes learning much more efficient."]
    end
```
